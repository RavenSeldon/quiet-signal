EVIDENCE · ABOUT THIS SPECIFIED MODEL

Four worlds, fixed before the run

Every pre-registered fixture held exactly. In the Funeral Bell, Information first and Static value both wait for the perfectly true bell. It rings at tick 4, one tick after the last reversible moment, and both lose protection (net −2.60, exactly one late truth per episode). Deadline-aware value takes the slow survey, which lands at tick 3 = D (−1.05). The Early-stop rule takes the fast scout (−1.85). Moving the bell to tick 3 turns the same truth protective: Information first improves from −2.60 to −0.85.

Across 1,323 configurations of this family

The family varies the deadline from 0 to 6, the bell's delay from 1 to 7, the prior, cost and budget. Deadline-aware value never lost to Information first or Static value. It won 681 configurations and tied 642. Every win came where the bell rang after the deadline. In this family, deadline-aware value also equalled the exact optimum everywhere. The simple Early-stop rule tied the optimum in 387 configurations (29%) and never beat it. Information first and Static value behaved identically here, because both always chose the perfect bell first. This family cannot tell those two apart.

Then we tried to prove ourselves wrong

An adversarial search over 193,536 wider configurations varied every sensor's reliability and delay. Deadline-aware value fell below the exact optimum in 4,227 (2.18%), by at most 0.222. The largest case is shown as world 05. Neither of two weak warnings changes the decision alone; together they do. A one-step policy stays silent; the exact optimum listens twice. QS—001's complementarity lesson returns with a clock. Knowing the deadline is not the same as looking ahead.

QUESTION · WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

  • The deadline is known exactly. Real deadlines are uncertain, and an agent must not be handed one it could not know. That variant is the next warranted experiment.
  • The model has one hazard, one protective action and one decision. There are no repeated decisions or partial protection, and the deadline is a hard step rather than a decaying chance.
  • Optimal dominance follows from knowing the true model. It is not evidence of a deployable advantage.
  • Utility counts only this harm. A late truth can still serve accountability, learning or someone else downstream. This model gives it no value by design, and says so instead of hiding it.
  • Related ideas exist. Delayed value of information is studied in decision theory, and early-warning practice distinguishes disseminating a warning from enabling protective action. The contribution here is a small, exact, replayable combination: bounded attention, deadlines and truthful-but-late warnings. It is not a new theory.
  • No claim about human attention, clinical practice, emergency management or real institutions.

CREATION · WHERE IT SITS

Sīdus remains the research lineage on whether accessible information shapes behaviour; nothing here amends it. The Seventh Seat's question of standing is a different failure: a warning can be timely, yet reach no one empowered to act. First Notice remains a separate operational protocol. The Hatchery companions are presentation, never evidence.

Sometimes the wise choice is to act immediately. Sometimes it is to wait. Sometimes the opportunity has passed, and the truth is kept for someone who can still use it. Truth does not stop mattering when prevention becomes impossible; its purpose changes.
